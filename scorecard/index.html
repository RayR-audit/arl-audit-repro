Allocation Readiness Level — Public Scorecard

An evidence-linked assessment of whether agentic-payment protocols carry rules for dividing the value they move. Maintained by Ray R, Independent Researcher.

Scope: x402 only (payment-execution layer). Additional protocols and layers will be added in future revisions. Assessments are version-stamped and may be revised as evidence accumulates. Every figure describes the frozen Dec 2025 – Apr 2026 window of the upstream CC0 dataset, not a live network.
x402 · Allocation dimension: ARL 0
"No allocation rule exists; value is captured by whoever controls the interface." — by the first question it fails
Snapshot 2026-09-07
Full audit: github.com/RayR-audit/arl-audit
Read this correctly. ARL 0 on allocation is not a verdict that x402 fails at its own job. As a payment execution layer, x402 is genuinely strong: fund safety by design, no single mandatory facilitator, multi-network reach, verifiable on-chain settlement volume (see the replication section for what that volume does and does not evidence). The finding is scoping, not condemnation: x402 answers who pays whom and leaves who deserves what share to layers it does not govern. That residual question is what this scorecard tracks.

The ARL scale (public 0–5)

LevelCriterion
0No allocation rule exists; value is captured by whoever controls the interface
1An allocation rule exists but is unverifiable (fails C1: external checkability)
2Verifiable but single-party only (fails C2: multi-party compatibility)
3Multi-party but capital-fused (fails C3: resistance to capital capture)
4Capture-resistant but decoupled from realized value (fails C4: economic value connection)
5Verifiable, multi-party, capture-resistant, and economically anchored

Classification rule: a system is classified by the first question it fails, not forced onto the nearest rank. ARL is an analytical scale, not a certification standard.

Independent measurement — aggregate findings

As a first step toward open audit infrastructure, we replicated population-scale measurement from an orthogonal direction on the Base-chain settlement slice of a public CC0 BigQuery listing: 52,652,290 settlements routed through 105 known facilitator addresses (99.86% of 52,723,702 rows in the raw listing). Events span 2025-12-05 to 2026-04-14 (UTC, 131 days) — the period covered by the upstream dataset, which has not been updated since April 2026. Concentration alone proves nothing — the question is whether the concentrated part behaves like an operator or like many independent customers. The rhythm and ticket-size gaps above indicate that genuine, consumption-driven demand is materially lower than settlement counts suggest: settlement count measures manufacturability, not demand (gas is sponsored, and nothing on-chain marks who controls a payment). Method & limitations

  • This page reflects a version-stamped snapshot (2026-09-07 sources). Conclusions expire with the snapshot; audits are re-snapshotted against future protocol versions.
  • "ARL 0" here applies to the allocation dimension of the payment-execution layer. It does not measure execution-layer quality, security, or adoption.
  • Standards are derived from the audited protocol's own published claims, not from an external rubric imposed on it.
  • Evidence marked "insufficient" was not treated as absence. G4 remains open until evidence is provided.
  • Absence findings are scoped to reviewed material. If an allocation primitive exists elsewhere in the spec, we want to know.
  • This methodology is v0.1 and itself expected to change.

Corrections welcome. Misquoted claims, wrong versions, or missed evidence — open an issue or PR on the audit repository. Corrections are merged with attribution.
