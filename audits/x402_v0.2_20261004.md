# x402 Allocation Readiness Audit — v0.2

> **Version-stamped snapshot**: This audit is based on the x402 documentation and repository as of **2026-09-07**, with incremental evidence added **2026-09-13** (specification v2, all nine published extensions, three payment schemes, `TSC.md`, and the Technical Charter; repository tree fully enumerated at 3,919 paths). The protocol evolves quickly; conclusions expire with the snapshot. We invite corrections — see [Contributing](../README.md#contributing).

**Ray R · Independent Researcher · v0.2 · 2026-10-04**

**Layer: payment execution** (value transfer). This audit asks one question of x402: when value flows through the protocol, does it carry any rule for how that value is *divided* among the parties that produced it — humans, agents, data providers, models, infrastructure? Attribution (who paid, who executed) is well addressed; allocation is the gap. We do not rank x402 against authorization-layer protocols (e.g. AP2): different layers, separate audits (see Appendix A for the layered mapping).

## 3. ARL assessment (allocation dimension)

> Classification rule (from the methodology): a system is classified **by the first question it fails**, not forced onto the nearest rank.

**x402, in the value-allocation dimension: ARL 0.**

- **ARL 0** — "No allocation rule exists; value is captured by whoever controls the interface"
- Basis: x402 defines who *pays* and who *executes* (client → resource server, via facilitator), and defines this well. It defines nothing about how value produced jointly by humans, agents, data, models, and infrastructure should be *divided*. With no allocation rule, the division defaults to whoever controls the interface (resource server pricing, facilitator fee-setting) — precisely the ARL 0 condition.

**Read this correctly.** ARL 0 on allocation is not a verdict that x402 fails at its own job. As a payment *execution* layer, x402 is genuinely strong: fund safety by design (X4, X12), no single mandatory facilitator (X7), multi-network reach (X5), signed commercial evidence emerging (X10), verifiable on-chain settlement volume (see §5
for what that volume does and does not evidence). The finding is scoping, not condemnation:
**x402 answers "who pays whom" and leaves "who deserves what share" to layers it does not
govern.** That residual question is what this project tracks.

## 4. Gap list (what would raise the allocation-dimension level)

| Gap | To reach | Description |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | ARL 1 (rule exists) | Any normative allocation rule or primitive: a value-split scheme, an allocation constraint type, a revenue-division extension point in the spec. Currently absent (scoped to reviewed material, snapshot 2026-09-13). |
| G2 | Facilitator-layer economics | **Closed for the incumbent facilitator** (public CDP pricing, X9); open for the ecosystem: whether a market of facilitators with divergent fee structures emerges, and whether fee-setting remains verifiable as the ecosystem scales. |
| G3 | ARL 2→3 | If an allocation rule is added: it must be independently verifiable and usable by heterogeneous parties (human sellers, agent operators, data licensors) — not a single operator's private ledger. |

## 5. Ecosystem reality check: settlement authenticity (case study)


Agentic commerce — AI agents paying for APIs, data, and compute autonomously — has been
declared "arrived" based on headline metrics: 160+ million settlements on the x402 protocol,
backed by Coinbase, Cloudflare, Google, Stripe and Visa under Linux Foundation governance.

Population-scale measurement tells a different story. The first academic audit
(arXiv:2607.12575, July 2026 — Ling et al., population-scale measurement of x402 on Base) found that of 136.7M settlements ($44.1M) on Base over 280 days:

- 21.20% are provably fictitious;
- 63.78% are internal settlement within linked clusters (self-dealing);
- genuinely independent demand is bounded between $187,861 and $20,258,746 — a 100x uncertainty
  interval that no current infrastructure can narrow.

Settlement count, the ecosystem's headline metric, measures *manufacturability*, not adoption:
gas is sponsored, nothing on-chain marks who controls a payment, and a manufactured settlement
is indistinguishable from genuine demand at the single-transfer level.

Security research compounds the gap. The first systematic study of 15 real-world facilitators
(USENIX Security 2026, covering 60K sellers / 360K buyers) demonstrates four novel attack
vectors: Free Shopping, Asset Theft, Service Denial, and Gas Abuse. Meanwhile price integrity
fails independently of fraud — 4.4% of 4,251 catalogued routes quote a live price that differs
from the listed price, with tail cases off by 100-1000x (TOLL·402 price-integrity study, August 2026).

### Why this matters beyond crypto

Agentic commerce is inheriting the internet's trust infrastructure from scratch — without the
consumer protection layer that took decades to build around card payments. Journalists and
legal analysts (Forbes, Bloomberg Law, Mondaq, August 2026) have already flagged the
chargeback liability gap: when an AI agent buys the wrong thing, no one can prove who
authorized what. The x402 case demonstrates a pattern this project has documented across
the AI economy: **adoption metrics that cannot be manufactured are the precondition for trustworthy
ecosystems — and they do not yet exist here.**

### What independent audit infrastructure would look like (research agenda)

1. Reproducible authenticity scoring (payment-graph clustering + cohort signals, per
   arXiv:2607.12575's method, as open tooling rather than a one-off paper);
2. Facilitator security benchmarking (extending x402scope's attack taxonomy);
3. Price-integrity monitoring (catalogued vs. live quote drift, per TOLL·402);
4. Agent reputation audit bridging ERC-8004 registries to independent verification.

This section will be updated as this project's related open-source work progresses.

### Orthogonal replication check (September 2026)

As a first step toward open audit infrastructure, we replicated the population-scale
methodology from an orthogonal direction and extended it, on the Tektonic x402 CC0 public
dataset (BigQuery, Analytics Hub). The public listing returns 52,723,702 raw settlement
rows; filtering to our 105-address facilitator registry retains 52,652,290 settlements
(99.86%), over the listing's full coverage window 2025-12-05 to 2026-04-14 UTC (131 days).
The upstream listing stopped updating in April 2026, so every number in this section
describes a frozen window, not today's network. As in the upstream note, every figure below
is a share of this audited slice, never of x402: Solana-side settlement is not covered, the
105-address registry is a hand-assembled scope condition rather than ground truth, and
facilitator-side internal traffic (routing, retries, batching) is indistinguishable from
customer traffic in the raw events.

Findings (aggregate statistics only; full addresses withheld; our companion note — Zenodo
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22807288 — reports the headline bound; the full parameter grid,
sensitivity analysis, and CC0 replication files ship with the final report):

- **Concentration confirmed on an independent pipeline.** Across payer-level totals in the slice, the Gini coefficient is **0.974 for settled value and 0.967 for settlement count** — two different measurements, reported together so neither label drifts. Both are near the theoretical maximum.
- **Coordinated payer cohorts dominate count, not value.** Behavioral fingerprinting with
  union-find clustering — using no funding graph at all, an approach orthogonal to the
  paper's funding-provenance method — identifies 181 coordinated payer cohorts covering
  52,008 of 78,196 payer addresses (66.5%). These cohorts account for **at most 84.1% of
  settlement count at the primary cutoff (fingerprint merge threshold ≥3 wallets; cohort
  cutoff ≥50 wallets)** (84.1–85.1% across the reported cutoff grid;
  74.3–74.4% at the widest) **but only 24.9% of settled value**. The mean settlement across
  the slice is $0.170: settlement count is the cheapest of the standard metrics to manufacture.
- **Orthogonal methods converge.** Ling et al. (arXiv:2607.12575, 2026-07-14) report 84.98%
  of Base settlement count as operator-internal; our behavioral-fingerprint bound is 84.1%,
  measured on a slice that is 38.5% of their census by count and ~20% by value. The
  fingerprinting and clustering are our own and use no funding graph — we did not use their
  data or code — but both analyses rest on the same underlying evidence (Base on-chain
  events), so this is convergence of methods, not fully independent replication.
- **Relation to concurrent industry measurement.** TRM Labs (Hodge, 2026-09-09,
  trmlabs.com/trm-tech-blog/whos-actually-paying-measuring-ai-agent-payments-onchain)
  report roughly USD 52.7 million across 198.9 million settlement transactions through
  known x402 facilitators since May 2025 across Base, Solana, and Polygon; after removing
  self-payments, bulk flows, and thin-buyer sellers, they bound the share that appears
  agentic at 0.6%-7.5% of the remaining USD 25.62 million. We did not use their data or
  code, and the methods differ (their screens plus agency bounds; our behavioral
  fingerprints on a frozen CC0 BigQuery window). Two cautions: the near-coincidence of
  headline figures — their USD 52.7 million of value vs our 52,652,290 settlements — is a
  coincidence of units and windows, not a cross-validation; and our audited slice is
  narrower than their population (105-address registry, 131-day window, no Solana side).
  The directional findings agree: both measurements conclude that headline settlement
  volume overstates genuine, consumption-driven demand.

## Appendix A. Layered mapping: the authorization layer (AP2) and the execution layer (x402)

> Self-contained section. Snapshot 2026-09-13 (AP2 repository `google-agentic-commerce/AP2`,
> main HEAD `e1ea56db72a6385bce3e5c1112b3a56ce60acb43`; specification.md titled "Agentic
> Payment Protocol (v0.2)"). This appendix maps layers; it does **not** rank across them.

**Why a mapping and not a ranking.** AP2 and x402 occupy different layers and describe
themselves accordingly. AP2 self-describes as: "The Agentic Payment Protocol (AP2) provides
a protocol to secure Agent-performed payment transactions" (specification.md, snapshot
2026-09-13), and states: "AP2 operates as a security feature within a Commerce Protocol. The
exact details of the Commerce Protocol … are outside the scope of AP2. AP2 is designed
explicitly to be compatible with the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) and integrates
seamlessly." (specification.md). It is also "agnostic to the particular payment instrument
used" — payment execution is left to the integrated party. x402, conversely, executes
payments and declares "Client-side budget management" out of scope (X11). Authorization
intent collection is likewise outside AP2's scope: "The Checkout and Payment Mandate contents
are assembled by the Shopping Agent after it has determined what task the user wishes it to
perform. The exact details of how this is achieved is outside of the scope of this
specification." (specification.md). **Complementarity is therefore not an editorial analogy: each specification scopes itself
that way — AP2 leaves payment execution to the integrated party, and x402 declares
client-side budget management out of scope.**

**What AP2 adds that x402 does not have.** Three verbatim-anchored points:

1. **Authorization credentials.** "Mandates are the core means that AP2 uses to authorize
   agents." AP2 defines Checkout and Payment Mandates with a constraint enumeration
   (allowed payee, allowed payment instrument, amount range, budget, recurrence, and others;
   docs/ap2/payment_mandate.md). x402's v2 specification contains no mandate concept
   (zero grep hits in the reviewed files); its only adjacent primitive is the `upto`
   amount cap (X12), with no payee, purpose, or credential constraint.
2. **A human-consent anchor.** "The Trusted Surface role is a UI surface that is trusted to
   get informed user consent for an Intent before creating a user-signed Mandate", and
   "The following role MUST be non-agentic: - Trusted Surface" (specification.md). x402 has
   no corresponding role or non-agentic requirement.
3. **A credential chain for disputes.** "In the case of a dispute, the Checkout Mandate and
   Receipt, and Payment Mandate and Receipt can be brought together to provide a
   non-repudiable picture of the transaction. Specific details of how this is used for
   dispute resolution, retention, and retrieval requirements are outside of the scope of
   this specification." x402's Offer and Receipt extension (X10)
   reaches the same dispute-evidence use case with server-side signatures, but those
   signatures carry no user-intent authorization, form no chain, and are self-declared
   not wire-stable. What x402 does that AP2 does not: it moves value (AP2 is agnostic to
   the payment instrument and delegates execution), with `upto` bounding the amount at
   verification time.

**What the mapping does not establish.** Integration between the two protocols is documented
only at the documentation and sample layer, in one direction, in the future tense: the AP2
FAQ answers "Does this work with x402 standard for crypto payments?" by describing AP2 as
payment-agnostic and pointing to a separate repository (`google-agentic-commerce/a2a-x402`)
as "an implementation of A2A in conjunction with the x402 standard," adding "We will be
aligning this closely with AP2 over time to make it easy to compose solutions which include
all payment methods, including stablecoins." The AP2 specification and README do not mention
x402 (zero grep hits in the reviewed files), and x402's reviewed materials do not mention
AP2 (zero grep hits). No claim of protocol-level support in either direction is supported by
the reviewed evidence.

**The allocation finding is shared.** In the value-allocation dimension — this project's
primary question — both protocols return the same answer: no allocation rule. A verbatim
grep across the full AP2 documentation set (11 files plus README and CONTRIBUTING) finds no
value-allocation primitive ("allocation", "revenue", "royalty": zero hits in allocation
semantics; the only "split" hits refer to order splitting and phase splitting). The same
holds for x402 (§2). AP2 does expose extension points that could host an allocation
constraint type ("To define a new constraint, the following MUST be specified: - A uniquely
defined `type`. - A Schema, including which fields are selectively disclosable. - The
evaluation algorithm."), but none is defined in v0.2. The precise joint finding is therefore:
**the authorization question has protocol designers; on
the evidence we reviewed, the allocation question has none.**

**Evidence-insufficient items (stated, not hidden).** AP2's governance sits with FIDO
Alliance ("The core specification work will continue in FIDO" — docs/faq.md); the working
groups' membership and admission criteria are not locatable in the repository and were not
independently verified at snapshot date. AP2 deployment scale at the payment-instrument
level is likewise not independently checkable from public sources we reviewed. We likewise
cannot determine whether AP2 carries an official allocation roadmap or constraint proposal
outside its repository; if one exists, this appendix's finding should be read alongside it.

## 6. Method & limitations

- Sources: x402.org (homepage, FAQ), the x402-foundation/x402 GitHub repository (README, main HEAD), docs.x402.org (facilitator page), the Coinbase Developer Platform x402 facilitator documentation (pricing) as of snapshot 2026-09-07; and at snapshot 2026-09-13: `specs/x402-specification-v2.md`, the three scheme specifications under `specs/schemes/`, all nine extensions under `specs/extensions/`, `specs/transports-v2/a2a.md`, `TSC.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, and the Technical Charter PDF — with the full repository tree enumerated (3,919 paths). - Absence findings are scoped to reviewed material. The x402 `specs/` tree contains network variants of the reviewed schemes that were not read line-by-line; if an allocation primitive exists there or elsewhere in the spec, this audit wants to know (open an issue).
- "ARL 0" here applies to the **allocation dimension of the payment-execution layer**. It does not measure x402's execution-layer quality, security, or adoption, and it is not a cross-layer comparison with AP2 (see Appendix A).
- Evidence marked "insufficient" was not treated as absence. G4's residual is stated with its cause (charter-referenced governance repository not locatable in the reviewed tree). G2 is closed for the incumbent facilitator (public pricing) and open for the broader facilitator ecosystem.
- This methodology is versioned and itself expected to change; audits will be re-snapshotted against future protocol versions. Findings added after 2026-09-26 go into a v0.3 addendum, not into this document's body.
